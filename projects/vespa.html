An icon, redrawn in pixels.

As Product Designer, I helped shape the digital evolution of the Vespa dashboard — refining the visual language of one of the most recognizable icons in design history. The goal: update the cockpit for a new generation of riders without losing what 75 years of identity built.

Designing inside a 75-year-old language.

Vespa is one of the most recognizable objects in the world. Since 1946, every generation has updated the scooter without ever betraying the silhouette, the proportions, the round headlight, the metal dashboard ring. The brand isn't just on the product — the product is the brand. Working on Vespa's digital cockpit means accepting a constraint that no brief can soften: nothing can look out of place, nothing can feel new for the sake of being new. Every pixel has to belong to the family.

Don't reinvent. Refine.

The brief was simple to say and difficult to execute: bring the dashboard into the digital era without changing the impression it leaves. The metal ring around the display, used on previous Vespa models, had to stay — it's part of the silhouette riders recognize before reading anything. The typography, the icon style, the color logic had to feel inherited, not designed. So the approach was inverted from a typical product project: instead of starting from a blank canvas, we started from the previous dashboard and earned every change. If a refinement couldn't be justified in terms of legibility, function or coherence, it didn't ship. Refinement, not redesign.

  • 0 changes to the iconic silhouette — the metal ring of previous models was preserved by design
  • Every change earned its place checked against legibility and function, and validated with the client to make sure it stayed true to the story riders already knew, before shipping
  • Previous dashboard as starting point — refinement, not blank-canvas redesign

Same family. One generation later.

The previous Vespa dashboard set the language. The new one carries it forward — same proportions, same identity, sharper details. The rider transitions between generations without learning anything new. That's the point. This design is now shipping in production — see it on the current Vespa GTS Supertech dashboard.
